Attic Water Damage: What Franklin Homeowners Should Know

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. In Franklin, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • Bubbling or peeling ceiling paint in a room on the top floor
  • Insulation that looks matted, flattened, or darker than the insulation around it
  • Attic ducts and metal hangers showing drips, rust or water marks
  • Streaks or dark staining on the roof deck's underside and on the rafters
  • Musty odor in upstairs closets or in the attic itself
  • Nail points coming through the sheathing that are rusty or beaded with water
How We Fix It

How We Handle Attic Water Damage

1

Inspect and Find the Source

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Wood

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. We clean visible mold off the wood, then apply an antimicrobial.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

A few weeks on

Ceiling Stains Appear

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

After months

Rot Sets In

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.

Water Near Electrical

Attics hold junction boxes, recessed light housings and cable runs. Water in or on them is a shock and fire risk, so stay out until the power is checked.

Lost Insulation Value

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

Mold on Sheathing

With poor ventilation, mold commonly grows on the bottom of the roof deck. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.
